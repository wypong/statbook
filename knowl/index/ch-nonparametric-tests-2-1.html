The tests in the previous chapter mostly had a familiar shape: state a hypothesis about a mean, proportion, variance, or table relationship; compute a standardized statistic; and compare it with a known reference distribution. Many of those methods are parametric tests. That means the model is described by a small number of population parameters, such as \(\mu\text{,}\) \(\sigma\text{,}\) or \(p\text{,}\) and the test usually relies on assumptions about the population distribution.
