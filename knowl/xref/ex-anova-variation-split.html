Example 12.1.2. Splitting Total Variation.

Three teaching methods are compared using quiz-score gains. Each method is used with four students.
🔗
The grand mean is
🔗
\begin{equation*} \overline{x}_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{6+8+7+9+10+11+9+10+13+15+14+16}{12} = 10.667. \end{equation*}
The between-group part is
🔗
\begin{equation*} B=(7.5-10.667)^2+(10-10.667)^2+(14.5-10.667)^2 \approx 25.167. \end{equation*}
Since each group has \(n=4\) observations, the between contribution to total variation is
🔗
\begin{equation*} nB=4(25.167)\approx 100.667. \end{equation*}
The within-group variation is
🔗
\begin{equation*} W=5+2+5=12. \end{equation*}
So the total variation should be
🔗
\begin{equation*} T=nB+W\approx 100.667+12=112.667. \end{equation*}
Most of the variation here comes from the group means being far apart, not from large spread inside the groups.
